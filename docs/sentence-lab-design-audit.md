# Sentence Lab drills lines, not analyst work yet

Sentence Lab's core loop is sound: you write a line, real Python runs it, and the app explains what went wrong. That is the right engine for fixing a production freeze. As written, though, the doc will not make you able to do real analyst and ML work, for four reasons. First, the curriculum covers about **8 of roughly 60 analyst/ML skills fully and 17 partly**, with no loading, inspection, dates, visualization, preprocessing pipelines, metrics, cross-validation, debugging or composition format in any version. Second, the ladder spends most new-note time on recognition tasks you already master, and it moves a note into spaced review after one scaffolded tile answer. Third, the "port verbatim" checker wrongly rejects correct answers (NaN, numpy scalar types, tree models, iterators, float rounding), sometimes including the reference line typed back exactly. It also wrongly accepts the very "dropped result" trap the app exists to teach. Fourth, the doc has no plan for review backlogs or missed days, which is the documented way spaced-repetition users quit. Three items block the handoff outright: the "20 checker cases" do not exist in the doc, "port verbatim" contradicts the `+=` labelling rule, and Validate is told to run distractor fragments as Python. Every fix below costs less now than after decks are written and review history builds up. The most important changes are: open new notes with a production attempt; graduate a note only after you produce it unaided; add a prerequisite-gated, unit-based curriculum with out-of-app capstones; replace the checker's single `same()` with per-note comparison rules; and add a load governor plus a skill-level progress view.

How to read this: severity and importance ratings are the panel's opinion unless a source is cited. "[K]" marks a figure a researcher recalled from memory and did not re-check, so verify it before relying on the number. "Background knowledge" marks technical claims that were not checked against documentation. Product and job-market sources dated before 2023 are flagged where they appear.

## 2. Proposed lesson plan: eleven units, each ending in a real-notebook capstone

The doc's success criterion is fewer freezes in real notebooks. The research found **no study showing that fluency on isolated lines transfers to composing multi-step analyses**. Tracing and reading are "necessary but not sufficient" for writing, and the authors "do not support the idea of a strict hierarchy" ([Venables et al. 2009](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/11384/1/2009002706OK.pdf)). In the closest controlled study to this app's format, Faded Parsons beat code-writing on pattern acquisition in both studies. On code-writing transfer it won in the first study (85.3% vs 74.7%) but tied in the second (27.7% vs 28.7%) ([Weinman et al. 2021](https://acelab.berkeley.edu/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/parsons-chi2021.pdf)). Every program surveyed ends in applied projects. So each unit below ends in a capstone done **outside the app**, in a real notebook. That respects §4's ban on notebooks inside the app.

**Mastery definitions (opinion, adapted from mastery-learning practice; none is validated for programming).**

- **Note mastered:** correct at L5 with no hint, no reference shown and the effect gate right, on at least 3 separate days with gaps of at least 1, 3 and 7 days. At least 2 of those 3 reviews use a different intent paraphrase or a variant with different names. On the last 2, time-to-first-keystroke is below your own rolling median.
- **Skill tag mastered:** at least 85% of the tag's notes are mastered, and you solve at least 2 of 3 never-seen probe notes at L5 on first exposure. The probes are what separate skill from memorised items.

The rest of the literature supports this design. Knowledge-tracing systems conventionally use P(mastery) ≥ 0.95, recent data favour 0.98, and N-in-a-row rules are criticised because one slip resets the count ([Zhang, Baker et al. 2025](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/edm25_short_bkt%20threshold_CR_final.pdf)). Successive relearning practises each item to criterion within each of several spaced sessions ([Rawson & Dunlosky 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09637214221100484)). A unit is mastered when its core tags are mastered and its capstone is finished in a real Jupyter notebook within a time box, using only official docs and no AI help. During the capstone you log each freeze (more than about 60 seconds before the first keystroke on a step).

A note on autograders (Part D). Established tools such as PrairieLearn, Otter-Grader and nbgrader compare values with tolerances and with strictness the author chooses per problem (dtype, order, names), not with one universal equality function. ## 4. Top 5 changes for learning and top 5 for engagement

The learning list is ordered by how much each change moves you toward working unaided in a notebook. The first two cost about one extra card per new note and probably shorten sessions, because most new notes would skip L0–L2. Two strong runners-up did not make the list: contrast-family interleaving (B5) and pre-check confidence (B9).

| Rank | Learning change | Gaps closed | Why it matters most |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnostic ladder: every new note opens at L5; a miss drops to the level of the failed stage | B1, B12, B4, B3 | It replaces four recognition trials per new note with production, which is the format with roughly twice the testing effect ([Rowland 2014](https://courseware.epfl.ch/assets/courseware/v1/fdde2f0aa590bf3b1324077a6bf1540c/asset-v1%3AEPFL%2BDEMO%2B2020%2Btype%40asset%2Bblock/Rowland2014-meta-analysis.pdf)) |
| 2 | Graduate on unaided production after a lag; master per skill tag with paraphrased intents and novel probes | B2, A17, B14 | Separates skill from memorising an intent → line pair, and matches successive relearning ([Rawson & Dunlosky 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09637214221100484)) |
| 3 | Prerequisite-gated unit curriculum, new card types (DBG, PAR, LEAK, SC, DOC) and out-of-app capstones | A1–A4, A9–A16 | Without this, finishing the app cannot mean job-ready |
| 4 | Elaborated feedback: name the diverging part, show the probe row, add a `rule` field, show the raw traceback first, then a correction retry | B7, B8, B10, A13 | Elaborated feedback beats answer-only feedback, g = 0.49 vs 0.32 ([Van der Kleij 2015](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314564881)) |
| 5 | Replace `same()` with per-note comparison rules and fix the false accepts | D4–D8, D11 | Wrong verdicts teach wrong rules and push correct notes into leech suspension |

The engagement list is built for one adult with a full-time job and grad school. It weights the documented failure mode of spaced repetition (abandoned backlogs) over engagement figures from Duolingo at massive scale.

| Rank | Engagement change | Gaps closed | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Load governor: review cap, automatic new-note throttle, about 5 new notes a day, catch-up mode, retrievability sort | C5, B18 | Backlog abandonment is the documented spaced-repetition failure ([Nour & Harris 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12662189)) |
| 2 | Competence map plus a three-line session debrief, in v1, off the review screen | C2, C3, C9 | Informational feedback is the one reward-like element that raises adult intrinsic motivation ([Deci 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)) |
| 3 | Weekly target with 2 reserve days in place of any streak (your call, since it reopens §2) | C4, C1 | Best evidence for bouncing back after a miss ([UCLA Anderson](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/)) |
| 4 | Session-level difficulty tuning plus an "I'm stuck" step-down | C6, B12 | Too-hard sessions drive quitting ([Birdbrain](https://spectrum.ieee.org/duolingo)) |
| 5 | Weekly field card from your own frozen notebook line, plus a freeze log on Home | C7, B14 | Ties the app to its own §1 success measure; supplies meaning with no extrinsic reward (opinion; no direct study) |

Avoid: daily loss-framed streaks (the streak becomes the goal itself, and breaks bring self-blame; [Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)); XP for activity, which would reward pressing Easy and grinding easy levels; leaderboards and mandatory badges (lower exam grades in [Hanus & Fox](https://www.smhp.psych.ucla.edu/pdfdocs/gamil.pdf), and meaningless for one user); notification optimisers, which are tuned for daily-active-user counts at scale; confetti and sounds; and a single mastery bar that crawls.
